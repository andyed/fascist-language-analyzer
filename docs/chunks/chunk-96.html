Theme: 13. Selective Populism

"Striking and even threats of bargaining and delay were considered acts against the people and thus improper."

Eco describes Selective Populism as a framework where 'the People' is conceived as a monolithic entity expressing a 'Common Will,' and any group that opposes the leader's interpretation of that will is delegitimized. By framing standard labor activities—such as bargaining and delay—not as legal rights or procedural disagreements, but as 'acts against the people,' the text treats a specific segment of the citizenry (federal employees) as being in opposition to the nation itself. This rhetoric subordinates individual or group rights to a singular, state-defined 'Voice of the People.'
