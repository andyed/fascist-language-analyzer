Theme: 13. Selective Populism

"The next Administration must not cede such authority to non-partisan “experts,” who pursue their own ends while engaging in groupthink, insulated from American voters. The following chapters detail how the next Administration can be responsive to the American people (not to entrenched “elites”); how it can take care that all the laws are “faithfully executed,” not merely those that the President desires to see executed; and how it can achieve results and not be stymied by an unelected bureaucracy."

This passage aligns with Eco's description of Selective Populism by framing 'the American people' as a monolithic entity whose will is uniquely channeled through the President and his political appointees. It characterizes independent institutional voices—specifically career civil servants and 'non-partisan experts'—not as legitimate components of a functioning democracy, but as 'entrenched elites' and an 'unelected bureaucracy' that 'stymies' the popular will. By demanding that all institutional authority be subordinated to the political will of the executive to remain 'responsive' to the people, it rejects the legitimacy of independent expertise and institutional checks.
